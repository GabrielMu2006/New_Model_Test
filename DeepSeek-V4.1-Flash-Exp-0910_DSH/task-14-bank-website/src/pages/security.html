If it happens

What to do in the first hour

Speed matters more than anything else. Most money lost to fraud is moved out within 60 minutes.

1

Lock the card or account

In the app: Cards → Lock. It takes three seconds and stops new charges immediately.

2

Call the fraud line

1-800-555-0177, 24/7. Have the last four digits of the card and the charge amount ready.

3

Change your password

From a device you trust. If you reused that password anywhere else, change it there too.

4

File a report

For identity theft, file at IdentityTheft.gov and put a freeze on your credit with all three bureaus.

Scams we see most

How to spot them in one line

"This is your bank — confirm this payment now"

We never call and ask you to read a code, move money to a "safe account", or install software. Ever. Hang up and call the number on the back of your card.

The tell: urgency plus a request to keep it secret.

"Your account is frozen — click here to verify"

Phishing emails copy our branding well but rarely our domain. Check that the sender is exactly meridian.example, not a lookalike. We won't send a login link you didn't ask for.

The tell: a link that goes somewhere other than the app you already have.

"You've won — just pay the fee"

Legitimate prizes never require a payment or a gift card. Neither does the IRS, the police, or a utility company. Any demand for gift cards is fraud, always.

The tell: paying money to receive money.

"Your new landlord asks for a deposit by wire"

Rental fraud peaks at the start of every month. Verify ownership through the county assessor's site and pay by a method you can reverse. Wire transfers are effectively final.

The tell: pressure to pay before you've seen the property in person.

"We're from tech support and found a virus"

Nobody from Microsoft, Apple, or your internet provider will cold-call you about a virus. If you've already given remote access, disconnect the machine and call us — we'll watch your accounts with you.

The tell: a stranger asking to control your computer.
